# Voyalier v0.9.0 — No route is a dead end

Voyalier 0.9.0 rebuilds the visa panel into a four-zone cockpit. Until now,
only four curated journeys existed (Canada's visitor visa and eTA, Japan's
short-term stay, Australia's electronic authorization) — every other route
ended in "check your destination's own immigration service," with no how. This
release makes every resolvable route a guided one, and adds the one number
travellers actually plan around, without Voyalier ever authoring it.

## Added

- **The universal playbook.** Any route without a curated journey now gets a
  six-step route map: finding the real authority under the lookalike agency
  sites, the shared vocabulary of entry paths, the document classes that fail
  when started late, where to file, how to track, and what a decision does and
  does not promise at the border. It opens with a banner naming it Voyalier's
  own writing — not read from any authority, not specific to the route — and
  it holds ADR-0006's no-fees-no-times-no-eligibility rule under a stricter
  build-failing scan than the curated prose. Ticks, notes, and the readiness
  tally work identically in both kinds of guide.

- **Published processing times, fetched — never bundled (ADR-0014).** Each
  named authority gets a statistics card under its own name, empty until you
  press fetch. One press reads IRCC's processing-times dataset or UKVI's
  decision waiting times page from your device — nothing about you or your
  trip is sent — and quotes the rows verbatim with the retrieval stamp, the
  source's own as-of date where published, the licence, and your passport's
  row marked where the publication is per-country. The raw page is kept
  locally and re-parsed on every read; a failed refresh keeps the copy visible
  under a loud banner, and copies older than a week say so. Authorities with
  no readable dataset (Japan, Australia, New Zealand, Korea, the United
  States) say exactly that, with the official link.

- **A passport picker that speaks your language.** Type a country's name in
  English or Spanish — accent-insensitive, ranked code match → name prefix →
  contains, each band alphabetically — or a bare two-letter code, exactly as
  before. A new trip offers the last trip's passport as one labelled tap
  instead of a silent prefill.

## Changed

- The visa panel's four zones sit on the app's new `--voy-space-*` spacing
  scale (the first consumer of the migration the token file promises), the
  missions list folds behind a disclosure, and headings no longer crowd their
  paragraphs.
- The readiness self-report now counts whichever guide renders — curated or
  playbook — still attributed to the traveller, still excluded from the
  rollup.
- Two rows joined the data-source register (IRCC processing times, UKVI
  decision waiting times), shown with licence and boundary lines like every
  other source.

## Documentation

- The visa guide page was three releases stale (it still described two curated
  destinations and a bare ISO field). Rewritten around the four zones, with
  the coverage, consent, and kept-copy stories matching the shipped product.
- ADR-0014 records the narrow amendment to ADR-0006's fetch rejection, the
  destination-keyed storage decision, the register additions, and the
  self-report change.

## Known limits

- Statistics parsers exist for two authorities; the other five are link-only
  by design. A US State Department parser and per-fetch history (trends) are
  deliberate future work — the stored copy is overwrite-on-refresh.
- The playbook links at most the one page a real curation act stands behind;
  it never invents portal or tracking URLs.
