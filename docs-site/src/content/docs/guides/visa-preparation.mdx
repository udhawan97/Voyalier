---
title: Visa and entry preparation
description: A step-by-step preparation cockpit that points at authorities and never speaks for them.
---

Voyalier does not decide whether you need a visa, and it never will. What it can
help with is the other half of the problem: not the rules, which governments
publish and change, but the execution, which nobody publishes and almost
everybody gets wrong the first time.

## What the guide is, and what it refuses to be

The split is absolute, and a test fails the build if it is ever broken:

- **Every factual claim about a requirement is a link** to the authority that
  published it.
- **Every sentence Voyalier writes is a translation or a caution** — what the
  authority calls something, what that means in plain language, or a specific
  way people commonly trip.

Voyalier never states a fee, a processing time, or an eligibility outcome, and
it never reports that your entry requirements are satisfied.

## Setting your passport

Open a trip and choose **Visa** in the section nav. Enter the two-letter country
code of the passport you will actually travel on (`IN`, `NG`, `GB`, and so on).
A new trip prefills the code from the last one you set, but never applies it for
you — a trip may not be for the same traveller.

Your nationality is encrypted at rest alongside your confirmations, and so is
every note you write against a document.

## Reading the quoted route

Once a passport is set, Voyalier shows the entry path **as a quote**, carrying
the authority that published it and the date the list was read:

> Visitor visa (temporary resident visa)
> Quoted from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada · read on 2026-07-28

Where a curated authority publishes conditions rather than a single answer, you
get that authority's official link and no journey. Voyalier will not fill that
gap with a guess.

Where the destination is not curated at all, there is no authority to quote, so
Voyalier names none and offers no link — it tells you to check your destination's
own immigration service. It will not stand a different country's government in
front of you because that is the one it happens to know.

## Working the steps

For a curated route, the cockpit lists the steps down the left and opens one at
a time on the right. Each step names what the authority calls the thing, then
explains it plainly, then lists the documents it takes — each with the specific
mistakes that cost people applications, and a link to the official page.

Tick a document when you have it. Add a private note if you want to remember
where you got to. Both persist per trip, so you can close Voyalier mid-application
and come back to where you were.

**Step one is a question Voyalier refuses to answer, and it is the most valuable
thing here.** Some authorities publish a far cheaper alternative to a full visa
for citizens of _some_ visa-required countries — Canada's electronic travel
authorization for travellers who have held a Canadian visa in the past ten years
or hold a valid US non-immigrant visa is the motivating case. The eligible list
is short and it moves. Voyalier puts the question in front of you with the list
attached, because a traveller who qualifies but never checks pays for a visa
they did not need.

## What this does not change

The **Entry requirements** item in [readiness](/Voyalier/guides/readiness-and-advice/) is
deliberately untouched. It still reads "Check yourself", it still asserts
nothing, and it still cannot clear your trip. It gains one line reporting your
own tally — attributed to you in the same sentence, because Voyalier has
verified none of it.

## Coverage

Two destinations are curated: **Canada** — both the visitor visa and the
electronic travel authorization routes — and **Japan**'s short-term-stay visa.
Other destinations get no quote, no link and no journey until they are curated —
an empty answer rather than a borrowed one. Each carries its own "read on" date,
because re-reading one authority says nothing about the other.

The two do not have the same shape, and that is the point. Japan publishes no
electronic authorization at all; its online visa service is a channel for the
same visa, and eligibility depends on the country you **live** in rather than the
passport you hold, so it appears as a link inside the journey rather than as a
door of its own. Japan also conditions many of its visa exemptions on a passport
type or a prior registration — Voyalier cannot see which passport you hold, so
those resolve to the ministry's own table and no journey, rather than to a guess
that could leave you at a gate without a visa.

The guidance is compiled into the app and stamped with the date it was read,
rather than fetched. That is not a shortcut: neither authority publishes a
machine-readable feed and both refuse automated requests, so a curated-and-dated
guide is the only honest shape. It works offline, and it tells you how old it
is.

**Always confirm your own case at the official source before you pay, book, or
travel.**
