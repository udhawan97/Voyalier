title: Visa and entry preparation
description: A four-zone cockpit that points at authorities and never speaks for them.
---

Voyalier does not decide whether you need a visa, and it never will. What it can
help with is the other half of the problem: not the rules, which governments
publish and change, but the execution, which nobody publishes and almost
everybody gets wrong the first time.

## What the guide is, and what it refuses to be

The split is absolute, and a test fails the build if it is ever broken:

- **Every factual claim about a requirement is a link** to the authority that
  published it.
- **Every sentence Voyalier writes is a translation or a caution** — what the
  authority calls something, what that means in plain language, or a specific
  way people commonly trip.

Voyalier never states a fee, a processing time, or an eligibility outcome, and
it never reports that your entry requirements are satisfied. The one place
numbers appear at all — the published processing times card below — shows only
figures your own device read from the authority's page, quoted verbatim with
the source, the retrieval time, and the licence attached.

## Setting your passport

Open a trip and choose **Visa** in the section nav. Start typing either the
country's name or its two-letter code — the picker suggests countries by name
in your own language, and a bare code (`IN`, `NG`, `GB`) always works exactly
as before. A new trip offers the passport from your last one as a single tap,
clearly labelled — offered, never applied, because a trip may not be for the
same traveller.

Your nationality is encrypted at rest alongside your confirmations, and so is
every note you write against a document.

## Reading the quoted route

Once a passport is set, Voyalier shows the entry path **as a quote**, carrying
the authority that published it and the date the list was read:

> Visitor visa (temporary resident visa)
> Quoted from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada · read on 2026-07-28

Where a curated authority publishes conditions rather than a single answer, you
get that authority's official link and no curated journey. Voyalier will not
fill that gap with a guess — the United Kingdom is the clearest case, covered
below. Where the destination is not curated at all, there is no authority to
quote, so Voyalier names none: it will not stand a different country's
government in front of you because that is the one it happens to know.

Neither case is a dead end any more. Wherever no curated journey exists, the
**universal playbook** takes its place.

## The universal playbook

Routes without a curated journey get a six-step route map — how to find the
authority and dodge the lookalike agency sites that outrank it, the shared
vocabulary entry paths are written in, the document classes that go wrong when
started late, where to file, how to track, and what a decision does and does
not promise at the border.

The playbook opens with a banner that tells you exactly what it is: **written
by Voyalier, not read from any authority, and not specific to your route.**
Every step tells you what to confirm at the official source, and where the
route has a named authority, the playbook links that authority's page and
nothing else. Its document items are cautions, deliberately conditional —
"photographs, _if they are asked for_" — because the official checklist is the
only list, and the playbook's job is to make sure the slow items on it do not
catch you late.

Ticks and notes work the same in both guides, and both count into the same
self-reported tally.

## Working the steps

The cockpit lists the steps down the left and opens one at a time on the
right. Each step names what the authority calls the thing, then explains it
plainly, then lists the documents it takes — each with the specific mistakes
that cost people applications, and a link to the official page.

Tick a document when you have it. Add a private note if you want to remember
where you got to. Both persist per trip, so you can close Voyalier
mid-application and come back to where you were.

**Step one of a curated journey is a question Voyalier refuses to answer, and
it is the most valuable thing here.** Some authorities publish a far cheaper
alternative to a full visa for citizens of _some_ visa-required countries —
Canada's electronic travel authorization for travellers who have held a
Canadian visa in the past ten years or hold a valid US non-immigrant visa is
the motivating case. The eligible list is short and it moves. Voyalier puts
the question in front of you with the list attached, because a traveller who
qualifies but never checks pays for a visa they did not need.

## Published processing times

For destinations whose authority is named, the cockpit carries a statistics
card headed by that authority's name. It starts empty on purpose: **no figure
is bundled with the app, ever** — a shipped number ages into a lie, and a
stale processing time is the kind that costs a booking.

Where the authority publishes its times in a form Voyalier can read — IRCC's
processing-times dataset for Canada, UKVI's decision waiting times page for
the United Kingdom — one press fetches them. The consent sentence beside the
button says exactly what happens: your device contacts that authority once to
read its published page, and nothing about you or your trip is sent, because
the page is the same for every traveller. What comes back is quoted verbatim
— the source's own labels, its own units, its own "no processing time
available" where that is what it says — with the retrieval time, the source's
own as-of date where it publishes one, and the licence line. Where the source
publishes per-country rows, the row for your own passport code is marked, and
the caption tells you to confirm it is yours.

The copy is kept on your device. When a later fetch fails, the kept copy stays
visible with a loud banner saying when it was saved; when any copy is more
than a week old, it says so and tells you to fetch again before relying on it.
Authorities that publish no readable dataset — Japan, Australia, New Zealand,
Korea, the United States today — get an honest sentence and the official
link instead of a broken fetch button.

## What this does not change

The **Entry requirements** item in
[readiness](/Voyalier/guides/readiness-and-advice/) is deliberately untouched.
It still reads "Check yourself", it still asserts nothing, and it still cannot
clear your trip. It gains one line reporting your own tally — attributed to
you in the same sentence, because Voyalier has verified none of it. With the
playbook, that tally now exists on every route, not only curated ones.

## Coverage

Three destinations carry a curated journey: **Canada** — both the visitor visa
and the electronic travel authorization routes — **Japan**'s short-term-stay
visa, and **Australia**'s electronic authorization. Each carries its own "read
on" date, because re-reading one authority says nothing about the others.

The journeys do not share a shape, and that is the point. Japan publishes no
electronic authorization at all; its online visa service is a channel for the
same visa, and eligibility depends on the country you **live** in rather than
the passport you hold, so it appears as a link inside the journey rather than
as a door of its own. Japan also conditions many of its visa exemptions on a
passport type or a prior registration — Voyalier cannot see which passport you
hold, so those resolve to the ministry's own table, and the playbook fills the
step-by-step gap without pretending to be Japan's answer.

**The United Kingdom is named as an authority and deliberately resolves no
curated route, and that is the design working rather than a hole in it.**
GOV.UK publishes no per-nationality table Voyalier could quote from; it
publishes an interactive questionnaire, because the answer turns on the
purpose and the length of your visit as much as on the passport you hold. Any
per-passport answer Voyalier printed would be a guess wearing a quote's
clothes. So a UK trip gets the authority named, a link to GOV.UK's own
[Check if you need a UK visa](https://www.gov.uk/check-uk-visa) checker, the
universal playbook labelled as Voyalier's own writing — and, one press away,
UKVI's own published waiting times.

Every other destination gets the playbook and honesty about the rest: no
authority named, no link invented, no borrowed government.

The guidance itself is compiled into the app and stamped with the date it was
read, rather than fetched — neither journey authority publishes a
machine-readable feed for its rules, so a curated-and-dated guide is the only
honest shape, and it works offline. The statistics are the deliberate
exception: read live from the authority's own publication at your request,
never shipped in the box (ADR-0014).

**Always confirm your own case at the official source before you pay, book, or
travel.**
